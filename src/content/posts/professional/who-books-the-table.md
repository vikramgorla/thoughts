In our team chat, someone writes "we should plan a team dinner soon".
Twelve people like it. Nobody books the table.

AI meeting notes now do the same, at scale.

Every meeting ends with a long list of action items. Nicely written, owned by nobody. Project managers and employees become mess creators, not finishers. Stanford and BetterUp have a name for this kind of output: workslop. It looks good, but has no substance.

Our CIO once said: **don't use AI to create more work for others.**

What works for me:

→ If AI or an agent can do the action, let it do it.
Don't assign it to a person.

→ If it really needs a human, put it in the team tracker.
Not in an email. One owner, one date, visible to the whole team.

→ Ask: if nobody touches it for a month, does it matter?
If not, don't create the action. One of our leaders taught me this.

→ Agree simple team rules.
For example: if nobody decides or responds in 3 days, you can decide on their behalf.

A summary is not progress. **A finished action is.**

How many action items from last month's meetings are still open?
