Friends plan a raclette evening. Everyone tells the host privately what they bring.
Three people bring cheese. Nobody brings potatoes.

One shared list fixes it. Even a friend who joins late just checks the list.

Work works the same way.

As my colleague Ruben Sanchez says, **emails and slides are personal**. They hide knowledge. Atlassian's own research found teams spend about a quarter of the week searching for information.

→ Document first, then announce.
GitLab calls it handbook-first. Write it in a shared place, then send the link.

→ A slide deck is a story for one meeting, not a record.
Put the decision and context on a Confluence page, and link the deck.

→ Agents can only use what is shared.
Microsoft 365 Copilot, Atlassian Rovo and Glean can read email, but only for the mailbox owner. **Your inbox helps only your copilot**. A shared page helps the team and its agents.

→ New joiners find the answer without asking.
A decision in Confluence or Jira can still be found next year, by people and agents.

Email is still right for external people and sensitive topics. For team knowledge, I prefer **the list everyone can read**.

Where does your team's knowledge live?
